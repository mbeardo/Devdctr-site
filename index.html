Made for the Mac

Written in SwiftUI. Light and dark mode, tiny on memory, and it sits in the menu bar until you need it. Prefer a proper window? One click, and you can make it the default.

Private by design

No account and no tracking. Everything is checked on your Mac. Doctor reads the names of keys in your .env files, never their values.

Careful with your stuff

Cleanup only touches folders your tools can rebuild, and moves them to the Bin. Domains asks for your password once, through macOS, and can be removed in one click.

Why I built it

Built out of a long-running annoyance.

For years, every working session started with a few terminal tabs and a string of commands just to find out where things stood: which server is on port 3000, did I push that branch, why is this project on the wrong version of Node? Debugging was worse, because the culprit was usually something small that was invisible until I went digging. So I built the thing I wanted: one place that shows everything important about my projects at a glance, one click away in the menu bar. I've found it genuinely useful every day, so I've put it out into the world, and I hope you will too.

MattDeveloper, and the person who made devdctr

Questions

Before you install.

Is the beta free?

Yes. The beta costs nothing and needs no card. When devdctr goes on sale, beta testers get a founding-member price: half price for the first year, or Lifetime for £49. Or you can simply stop using it.

Why does macOS ask before opening it?

Beta builds aren't notarised by Apple yet. The first time you open devdctr, macOS stops it; go to System Settings › Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway. You only do this once, and release versions will open without it.

Will Cleanup delete my projects?

No. It only removes folders your tools recreate, like node_modules, vendor, build output and caches. Your code, Git history and settings stay where they are, and everything removed goes to the Bin first.

Why isn't it on the Mac App Store?

App Store apps run in a sandbox that blocks what devdctr does, like seeing which processes hold a port or serving .test domains. It's sold directly instead. Release versions will be signed and notarised by Apple, so macOS opens them without warnings.

Does devdctr read my Claude conversations?

No. The AI tab reads only token counts, model names and folder names from Claude Code's files on your Mac. Your messages and code never leave your Mac, and devdctr sends nothing to Anthropic.

Does it need my admin password?

Only for Domains, once. macOS asks for it when you send .test addresses to your Mac and again when you trust the certificate. devdctr never sees your password.

Which Macs does it run on?

Any Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or later) running macOS 14 Sonoma or later. The beta doesn't run on Intel Macs yet.

How many Macs can I use it on?

Each licence works on up to 3 of your Macs. Replacing a Mac? Remove the old one from your licence and add the new one.

What happens when the beta ends?

Beta builds stop working on the day the beta ends, and the app shows a thank-you with your founding-member discount. You'll see a reminder in the app two weeks before, and we'll email you too.

Are updates really free?

Yes. While you're subscribed, or if you bought Lifetime, every update is included, new tools too.

What happens if I cancel?

devdctr keeps working until the end of the period you've paid for. Your projects are never affected, and you can come back whenever you like.

Will the price go up?

Not for you. Whatever you pay when you subscribe is what you keep paying for as long as you stay subscribed.

Does it send my code anywhere?

No. Every check runs on your Mac. devdctr only goes online to check your licence and look for updates, and, if you push code over SSH, to confirm GitHub, GitLab or Bitbucket still accepts your key.
